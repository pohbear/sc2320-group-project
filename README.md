# Congressional Trading Performance & Anomaly Detection

## Overview

This project investigates the trading activities of U.S. Congress members to determine whether they demonstrate an unfair informational advantage in the stock market. By analyzing public disclosure data (from Capitol Trades) alongside historical market data (S&P 500) and sector classifications, this project aims to uncover anomalous trading patterns and quantify the extent to which politicians outperform the broader market baseline.

The analysis is structured sequentially—progressing from raw data ingestion and feature engineering to statistical hypothesis testing and advanced machine learning modeling. Ultimately, the project seeks to identify which politicians exhibit the most statistically significant and highly unusual returns (using Wilcoxon tests and DBSCAN clustering) and attempts to predict future stock outperformance using a Random Forest Classifier. The results provide quantitative evidence regarding whether specific domain knowledge (such as congressional committee assignments) translates into market outperformance.

## Usage

This project is structured as a pipeline of numbered Jupyter Notebooks. To reproduce the analysis or explore the data, start a Jupyter Notebook server:

```bash

```

Navigate through the notebooks in sequential order, as later notebooks depend on the output files (saved to the `data/output/` directory) generated by the earlier ones:

1. `nb1_data_preperation.ipynb`
2. `nb2_eda.ipynb`
3. `nb3_feature_engineering.ipynb`
4. `nb4_hypothesis_test.ipynb`
5. `nb5_dbscan.ipynb`
6. `nb6_random_forest.ipynb`

## Methodology / Approach

1. **Data Preparation (`nb1_data_preperation.ipynb`)**: Gathers and merges raw Capitol Trades data, S&P 500 records, and base tickers. Handles parsing, indexing, and standardization of trades to produce clean tabular data.
2. **Exploratory Data Analysis (`nb2_eda.ipynb`)**: Examines trade distributions, volume sizes, and behavioral statistics to understand overall trading volume, sizes, and politician demographics.
3. **Feature Engineering (`nb3_feature_engineering.ipynb`)**: Calculates critical features such as anomalous returns (relative to the market benchmark), blind spot metrics, and rolling averages, building out the comprehensive `analytical_dataset.csv`.
4. **Hypothesis Testing (`nb4_hypothesis_test.ipynb`)**: Employs statistical tests (e.g., Wilcoxon rank-sum test) to determine if the trading returns of specific politicians differ significantly from market averages. 
5. **DBSCAN Clustering (`nb5_dbscan.ipynb`)**: Utilizes unsupervised DBSCAN clustering—along with PCA for dimensionality reduction—to identify distinct outlier politicians based on their trading profile and sector inclinations. It also cross-references these outliers with the hypothesis testing results to flag convergence politicians.
6. **Machine Learning Classifier (`nb6_random_forest.ipynb`)**: Trains a scalable PySpark Random Forest model to predict if a specific BUY trade will beat the S&P 500 over a 60-day window. Feature importances are extracted to understand the predictive power of variables like committee assignments and disclosure speeds.

## Key Findings / Results

* **Significant Outperformance:** The hypothesis tests single out a cluster of convergence politicians who not only exhibit statistically significant positive abnormal returns but are also flagged as massive behavioral outliers by the DBSCAN clustering algorithm.
* **Information Advantage Validation:** Based on the Random Forest feature importances, specific indicators (such as congressional committee assignments and blind-spot metrics) surface as highly predictive signals. For example, politicians trading within the sectors correlated heavily with their committee oversight exhibit noticeably advanced market timing.
* **Predictive Performance:** The PySpark Random Forest classifier successfully differentiated outperforming trades from market-average trades, securing an AUC-ROC that establishes a valid signal beyond random market noise (as summarized in `rf_results_summary.txt`).
